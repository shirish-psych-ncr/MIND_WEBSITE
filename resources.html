Simple tools for difficult moments

These guided exercises may help you pause, ground yourself, or refocus when a difficult moment builds. Use them only if they feel comfortable; they do not replace assessment or treatment.

Choose a tool for the moment you are in

Guided Breathing

A guided way to pause when anxiety, overthinking, or restlessness builds up. Follow visual cues and stay within a comfortable pace.

Start Exercise

Lateral Grounding

Keep your head still and follow the moving light with your eyes only. Stop if the movement feels uncomfortable.

Start Session

Fractal Grounding

Tap to explore a fractal, then hold and move to swirl. Match your breathing to the glow as a visual focus exercise.

Launch Tool

River of Release

Type a worry, watch it drift away on a leaf. A cognitive defusion exercise to help externalize and release anxious thoughts.

Release a Worry

Butterfly Tapper

Try a gentle alternating-tap visual exercise. Follow the comet at a comfortable pace and stop if it feels uncomfortable.

Begin Tapping

Horizon Scan

Use smooth eye movements across the horizon as a visual focus exercise. Stop if movement feels uncomfortable, especially during panic.

Start Exercise

When to seek professional help

  • Symptoms lasting weeks or getting worse
  • Sleep, appetite, or energy significantly affected
  • Difficulty functioning at work, school, or home
  • Frequent distress, panic, or emotional overwhelm
  • Concerns about safety, self-harm, or substance use

If you're unsure, that itself is a valid reason to consult a mental health professional.

Need more clarity?

If difficulties are persistent, worsening, or affecting daily life, a clinician can help you decide what support may be appropriate.
